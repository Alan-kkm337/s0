Devolatilization of polymers is a critical step in solution polymerization processes. The removal of residual solvent from concentrated polymer solutions determines both product quality and energy consumption. Conventional steam stripping requires large amounts of steam and generates significant wastewater. In contrast, dry devolatilization processes combine multistage flashing with vacuum extrusion to remove volatiles directly. The falling strand devolatilizer is widely used because it provides a large surface area for mass transfer. However the high viscosity of concentrated EPDM solutions limits the surface renewal rate and reduces the overall devolatilization efficiency. Recent studies show that adding a stripping agent can enhance the removal of residual volatiles in a vented twin screw extruder. The design of such processes requires accurate knowledge of the diffusion coefficient and the mass transfer coefficient in concentrated polymer solutions.
